# US macroeconomic outlook

**As of 10 October 2026 | Official-source context, researched separately from the demo**

The evidence points to resilient demand alongside persistent inflation and subdued hiring. This is an analytical interpretation, not a trading signal or a model-derived forecast.

**Inflation:** August headline CPI increased 0.4% month over month and 3.4% year over year; core CPI rose 0.3% monthly and 2.4% annually. Energy contributed to headline pressure. September CPI was not yet released at this cutoff; its scheduled release is 14 October. [BLS CPI, released 11 September](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/cpi_09112026.htm).

**Labour market:** September nonfarm payrolls increased by 29,000, unemployment was 4.2%, and hourly earnings rose 3.0% over the year. July and August payroll revisions lowered their combined job gains by 60,000. These readings suggest limited hiring momentum, although one report cannot establish a recession. [BLS employment report, released 2 October](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/empsit_10022026.htm).

**Policy:** On 16 September the FOMC raised the federal funds target range by 25 basis points to 3.75%-4.00%, citing elevated inflation. Restrictive policy and soft hiring create a tension between price stability and labour-market risk. [Federal Reserve statement](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260916a.htm).

**Activity and spending:** The third estimate put Q2 real GDP growth at a 2.2% annualized rate, up from the previous 1.5% estimate. August real consumer spending increased 0.6% monthly, while real disposable income was unchanged. Headline PCE inflation was 3.4% annually and core PCE 3.0%. Stronger consumption therefore coexists with pressure on household purchasing power. [BEA GDP, 30 September](https://www.bea.gov/news/2026/gdp-third-estimate-industries-corporate-profits-state-gdp-and-state-personal-income-2nd); [BEA income/outlays, 30 September](https://www.bea.gov/news/2026/personal-income-and-outlays-august-2026).

**Forward view - inference:** A continued expansion with inflation above the Federal Reserve's goal is a reasonable base scenario; hiring weakness raises downside risk. Hotter-than-forecast inflation could increase rate pressure, while weaker jobs and spending could shift attention toward growth risk. Their asset-price effects depend on positioning and expectations. The next CPI report and 29 October GDP/PCE releases are useful checkpoints, not known outcomes.
